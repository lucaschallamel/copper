# BD visual language

Enumerated reference for `qd-masters:master-bd-visual-language`. Consulted during a composition pass , the
shot, angle, expression, lighting and palette vocabularies, kept verbatim.

## Style influences

- **Ligne claire** (clear line): clean outlines, flat colours, balanced composition (Hergé, Joost
  Swarte)
- **Realist school**: detailed environments, naturalistic anatomy (Hermann, Jean Giraud)
- **Atmospheric / noir**: dramatic shadows, mood-driven palettes (Tardi, Enrico Marini)
- **Franco-fantastic**: stylised realism with expressive character design (Mathieu Bonhomme, Blain)

Public craft vocabulary , named practitioners and published schools, not project canon.

**Visual principles**: clarity over flash; expressive simplicity (maximum emotion, minimum lines);
environmental storytelling; colour as narrative; anatomical believability even when stylised.

## Shot types (distance)

- **ECU** extreme close-up: eyes, hands, object detail , maximum intimacy or focus
- **CU** close-up: face filling frame , emotion, reaction
- **MCU** medium close-up: head and shoulders , dialogue, character focus
- **MS** medium shot: waist up , action and interaction
- **MLS** medium long shot: full body , character in environment
- **LS** long shot: full figure with environment , establishing, context
- **ELS** extreme long shot: landscape, architecture , scope, isolation

## Camera angles

**Vertical**

- **Bird's eye / high angle**: looking down , vulnerability, insignificance, surveying
- **Eye level**: neutral , objective, natural
- **Low angle / worm's eye**: looking up , power, threat, monumentality

**Horizontal**

- **Frontal**: direct engagement, confrontation
- **Three-quarter**: natural, most common for dialogue
- **Profile**: side view, observation, separation
- **OTS** over-the-shoulder: POV, conversation dynamics
- **Dutch angle / tilt**: disorientation, unease, chaos

**Selection criteria**: narrative purpose (what emotion or information the panel conveys); visual
variety (avoid repetitive shots across consecutive panels); character dynamics (power shown through
angle); pacing support (closer = slower, wider = faster).

## Facial expression

- **Eyes**: pupil dilation, gaze direction, eyelid position (wide / narrowed / closed)
- **Eyebrows**: raised, furrowed, asymmetric
- **Mouth**: lip tension, teeth visible, corner position
- **Overall**: relaxed, tense, asymmetric , complexity reads as realistic emotion

**Emotion mapping**

- **Fear**: wide eyes, raised brows, open mouth, tense jaw
- **Anger**: narrowed eyes, furrowed brows, clenched jaw, flared nostrils
- **Surprise**: wide eyes, raised brows, open mouth
- **Sadness**: downcast eyes, inner brows raised, mouth corners down
- **Joy**: crinkled eyes, raised cheeks, mouth corners up
- **Contempt**: one-sided raised lip, narrowed eyes, head tilt

## Body language and blocking

- **Posture**: upright (confident), hunched (defeated), leaning (interested or aggressive)
- **Gesture**: hand position, arm cross, finger pointing
- **Weight distribution**: balanced, shifting, off-balance
- **Spatial relationship**: intimate / personal / social / public distance
- **Movement direction**: static, approaching, retreating, turning

**Blocking principles**: position for clear silhouettes; important characters in visual prominence;
eye-lines guide reader attention; action flows left-to-right in Western reading direction.

## Environment and depth

- **Architecture**: building style, materials, condition (pristine to decayed)
- **Interior**: furniture arrangement, wall decoration, floor type
- **Exterior**: vegetation, weather, urban or rural density
- **Depth layers**: foreground, middle ground, background
- **Scale indicators**: objects showing size relationships

## Lighting , where mood is created

**Light source**: natural (sun position, time of day, weather diffusion) or artificial (lamp,
candle, neon, firelight). Direction , front, side, back, top, bottom. Quality , hard (sharp shadows)
or soft (diffused).

**Lighting schemes**

- **High-key**: bright, minimal shadows , optimistic, cheerful (comedy, daytime)
- **Low-key**: strong contrast, deep shadows , dramatic, tense (noir, night)
- **Chiaroscuro**: extreme contrast, Renaissance drama , revelations, conflict
- **Silhouette**: backlit figures , mystery or danger
- **Rim lighting**: edge highlighting , separation from background

**Shadow patterns**: cast shadows (objects blocking light), form shadows (an object's own curves),
and shadow-direction consistency across the panel.

## Colour palette

- **Dominant hue**: sets emotional tone (warm or cool, saturated or desaturated)
- **Accent colours**: draw attention to important elements
- **Palette shifts**: scene-to-scene emotional transitions
- **Symbolic colour**: character-associated colours for visual continuity

**Approaches** , **monochromatic** (single hue variations, unified mood), **analogous**
(neighbouring hues, harmonious), **complementary** (opposite hues, visual tension), **triadic**
(three equidistant hues, balanced dynamism).

## Atmospheric effects

Weather (rain, fog, snow, heat haze); particles (dust, smoke, steam); depth cues (atmospheric
perspective , distant objects lighter and bluer); motion blur (subtle, not overdone).

## Focal hierarchy

**Primary focus** , central positioning, highest contrast, sharpest detail, character eye-lines
pointing toward it. **Secondary** , lower contrast, less detail, discovered second. **Background** ,
context without distraction, softer focus, atmospheric.

**Detail level**: foreground high detail with visible texture; middle ground moderate, main action
clarity; background suggested, atmospheric indication; **out-of-focus areas** , blur degree and
bokeh effects where depth of field is doing narrative work.

## Composition rules

- **Rule of thirds**: 3x3 grid, key elements at intersections, horizon on the top or bottom third,
  never centre
- **Leading lines**: architecture, roads and eye-lines guide the viewer , diagonals read as dynamic
  energy, horizontals as stability, verticals as strength
- **Framing within frame**: doorways, windows and architecture frame the subject, creating depth
- **Negative space**: empty areas balance the composition, rest the eye, and emphasise isolation
- **Visual weight**: asymmetric balance reads as more interesting; larger, darker and foreground
  objects carry more weight

## Quality bar

Non-negotiable , character visual consistency against profiles, lighting logic (sources and shadows
cohere), compositional clarity, emotional accuracy.

Excellence , cinematic shot variety, atmospheric mastery, expressive depth, image conveying meaning
without text, stylistic coherence across pages.
